# `data/`: test datasets and codebook

> **SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION DATA.** Every value in these files was generated by
> [`generate.py`](generate.py) from a seeded random model. None of it was observed at a real
> bikeshare station, and it must never be presented as real.

Three files in the format of a logged GBFS `station_status` feed, one row per station per
five-minute snapshot, rush windows only. They feed both decisions in [`SPEC.md`](../SPEC.md):
the availability p-charts (one per station and rush window) and the network maintenance chart.

| File | Stations | Rows | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| [`stable.csv`](stable.csv) | 10 (S01-S10) | 7,200 | a normal network: opposite residential and downtown rush patterns, one chronic hilltop station, a steady broken-bike share |
| [`planted_events.csv`](planted_events.csv) | 10 (S01-S10) | 7,200 | the same network with two planted signals after the baseline (below) |
| [`edge_case.csv`](edge_case.csv) | 2 (E01, E02) | 1,432 | a station that is never unusable, and missing snapshots |

Rows = stations x 15 weekdays x 48 snapshots (24 in each rush window); `edge_case.csv` is
2 x 15 x 48 = 1,440 minus 8 missing snapshots.

## Columns

The column names are GBFS's own (`station_status.json`), so a real log drops straight in.

| Column | Type | Units | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `timestamp` | text, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM` | local time (24-hour clock) | when the snapshot was taken; every 5 minutes, 07:00-08:55 and 16:00-17:55 |
| `station_id` | text | - | the station; `S01`-`S10` in the main files, `E01`-`E02` in the edge file |
| `num_bikes_available` | integer >= 0 | bikes | rentable bikes docked at the station. **0 = empty**: nobody can start a trip |
| `num_bikes_disabled` | integer >= 0 | bikes | broken bikes docked at the station; they take up a dock but cannot be rented |
| `num_docks_available` | integer >= 0 | docks | free docks. **0 = full**: nobody can end a trip |

A snapshot is **unusable** when `num_bikes_available == 0` or `num_docks_available == 0`.
For every row, `num_bikes_available + num_bikes_disabled + num_docks_available` equals the
station's capacity (below); no docks are out of service in this synthetic data.

## Calendar and baseline

- 15 weekdays, Mon **2026-09-14** to Fri **2026-10-02**, numbered day 1 to day 15. No weekends.
- **Baseline: days 1-10 (2026-09-14 to 2026-09-25)**, identical in `stable.csv` and
  `planted_events.csv`. Control limits are set from these days only.
- Rush windows: **AM** 07:00-08:55 and **PM** 16:00-17:55, 24 snapshots each.

## How the data were generated

Run from the repo root: `python data/generate.py` (or `--out <folder>` to write elsewhere).
Standard library only. **Seed: `SEED = 20261016`**, chosen once before any tuning; the station
parameters were tuned to the design above, the seed was never changed to get a nicer result.
`tests/test_data.py` regenerates the files and checks they are byte-identical.

For each station, weekday and rush window:

1. **Broken bikes for the day:** each of round(0.5 x capacity) bikes is broken with probability
   0.10. The broken bikes stay docked all day.
2. **Rentable bikes:** a bounded random walk over the 24 snapshots. It starts at a set share of
   the space not taken by broken bikes; at each 5-minute step up to 2 bikes arrive (each with
   probability `p_arrive`) and up to 2 leave (each with probability `p_depart`), and the count
   stays between 0 and capacity minus broken bikes. Residential stations drain in the AM
   (`p_depart` > `p_arrive`) and fill in the PM; downtown stations do the opposite. The exact
   parameters are in the `STATIONS` table in `generate.py`.
3. **Free docks** = capacity - rentable bikes - broken bikes.

Each (station, day, window) has its own random stream, seeded from `SEED` and its key, so
changing one day never changes another. That is what keeps the baseline of
`planted_events.csv` identical to `stable.csv`.

## Planted signals (`planted_events.csv` only)

| Signal | Where | How it was planted | What it looks like in the raw counts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stadium event** | S08, day 12 (Tue 2026-09-29), PM | from 16:10 on, every dock not taken by a broken bike is filled | S08 is full in 22 of 24 PM snapshots that day; 0 full PM snapshots on every baseline day |
| **Broken-bike surge** | whole network, days 13-15 (2026-09-30 to 2026-10-02) | the broken-bike probability doubles from 0.10 to 0.20 | the network share of broken bikes at 07:00 rises from 0.083 in the baseline to 0.169 (2.0x) |

Nothing else changed; `tests/test_data.py` checks that the two files differ only in these
rows. Because broken bikes take up docks and are no longer rentable, the surge also shifts the
rentable-bike and free-dock counts on days 13-15 at every station.

## Expected chart behavior

These numbers come from an **independent exact-binomial check** run while tuning the data
(alpha = 0.00135 in each tail, limits from the baseline, as in `SPEC.md` section 4). The H-03
function in `app/qc.py` must reproduce them; if it does not, one of the two is wrong and that
is worth knowing.

**`planted_events.csv`, the signals:**
- S08 PM on day 12: **flagged** (22 of 24 unusable against a baseline share of 0.083).
- Maintenance chart: days 13 and 14 **flagged** (29/159 and 29/157 broken); day 15 (22/158)
  rises but stays inside the limits.
- S07 AM: **not flagged** on any day; its share of 0.300 is above the 0.10 target, so the tool
  should say "capacity review", not "investigate".
- The surge also pushes a few stations' availability above their limits on days 13-15
  (S02 AM on days 13-14, S03 PM and S06 PM on day 13), because broken bikes take docks.

**`stable.csv`, the false-alert count: 14 of 300 availability points and 1 of 15 maintenance points flag,**
even though nothing was planted:
- availability: S02 AM day 15; S04 PM day 7; S05 AM days 10 and 13; S05 PM day 10; S06 AM day
  11; S06 PM days 5 and 6; S07 PM day 7; S08 PM days 9 and 13; S09 AM day 5; S09 PM day 3;
  S10 PM day 10;
- maintenance: day 7 flags **low** (3 broken of 149 docked bikes).

A truly binomial process would give about 300 x 0.0027 = 0.8 false availability alerts.
The excess is real and expected: an empty or full spell lasts several snapshots, so unusable
snapshots arrive in clumps and the day-to-day counts vary more than a binomial allows
(overdispersion). This is the known limitation in `SPEC.md` section 4, and why the tool labels
every signal **exploratory**.

**`edge_case.csv`:**
- E01: never unusable, so the baseline share is 0 in both windows and the tool must say
  "insufficient basis for control limits", not draw limits at 0.
- E02: 4 snapshots missing on day 3 AM (07:30-07:45) and 4 on day 11 PM (17:00-17:15), so those
  windows have n = 20 and their limits step up. E02 AM day 3 and E02 PM days 10 and 14 flag.

## Collecting this data for real

Every GBFS bikeshare system publishes a public `station_status.json` feed (listed in its
`gbfs.json` discovery file); it needs no account or key.

1. Every 5 minutes on weekdays, 07:00-09:00 and 16:00-18:00, a scheduled job (cron, or a
   scheduled Posit Connect job) downloads `station_status.json`.
2. For each station in it, write one row: `last_reported` (a Unix epoch time) converted to local
   time and rounded to the 5-minute mark, then `station_id`, `num_bikes_available`,
   `num_bikes_disabled` and `num_docks_available`, which are copied as published.
3. Each station's capacity is in `station_information.json`; a real feed also reports
   `num_docks_disabled`, which this tool does not use.
4. A missed download is simply a missing row, which the tool handles (it lowers n).
   Drop stations whose `is_renting` or `is_returning` is false during the window, and note them,
   since closed stations are a different problem from empty or full ones.

Two weeks of logging (10 baseline weekdays) is enough to set the first limits.
